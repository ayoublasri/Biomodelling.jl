# Methods

## Reaction model and propensities

A model is a set of species and reaction channels. Species are molecules (partitioned at division) or promoter states (inherited). For a reaction with reactant counts $x_i$, stoichiometric coefficients $\nu_i$, rate constant $k$ and volume exponent $e$, the mass-action propensity is
$$a = k\,V^{e}\prod_i \binom{x_i}{\nu_i},$$
where by default $e = 1-\sum_i \nu_i$, so that zero-order production scales with volume, first-order reactions are volume independent and bimolecular reactions scale as $1/V$; this keeps concentrations invariant under growth. Regulated reactions multiply this by $b + (1-b)F(\mathbf{c})$, where $b$ is a basal fraction and $F$ combines Hill terms of the regulator concentrations $c_j = x_j/V$, $h_j = c_j^{n_j}/(K_j^{n_j}+c_j^{n_j})$ for activators and $1-h_j$ for inhibitors, either as a product (AND logic) or as $1-\prod_j(1-h_j)$ (OR logic). Promoter switching is represented by explicit promoter species (for instance $G_{\mathrm{off}} \rightleftharpoons G_{\mathrm{on}}$) grouped into promoter groups. Custom propensities can be supplied as functions of counts, volume, parameters and time.

## Stochastic simulation kernels

Within one update step the cell volume and gene copy number are held fixed and the reaction network is advanced by one of four kernels: the direct method [@gillespie1977] with propensities updated through a species-reaction dependency graph; fixed-step Poisson tau-leaping [@gillespie2001]; a hybrid scheme in which a leap that would drive any species negative is discarded and that interval is simulated exactly instead (the "switching" scheme of version 1, with the discarded interval resolved exactly rather than clipped); and adaptive tau-leaping with the step-size selection of @cao2006 and the critical-reaction partition of @cao2005, falling back to a fixed number of exact events when the proposed leap is shorter than $10/a_0$. The hybrid scheme is approximate rather than exact: a leap is kept only when it leaves every species non-negative, so an accepted step follows the Poisson leap truncated to the non-negative region, and only the discarded intervals follow the exact process. The discrepancy falls with $\tau$ and is bounded by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test of Fig. 2d, which the scheme passes at $\tau = 0.05$ and fails at $\tau = 0.2$ for the models tested; the direct method is used wherever exactness is required (Supplementary Table 1 lists the kernel correctness tests). Every kernel takes an explicit random number generator.

## Cells, growth, replication and division

Each cell carries its volume $V$, age, generation, lineage identifiers, gene copy state, growth rate, molecule counts, a cell-specific parameter vector and its own random number generator (seeded from the parent's generator, which makes results independent of the number of threads). Volume grows exponentially, $\dot V=\lambda V$, with an optional log-normal spread of $\lambda$ between cells. Division is triggered by a sizer ($V \ge V_{\mathrm{div}}$), an adder ($V - V_{\mathrm{birth}} \ge \Delta$) or a timer (age $\ge T$), each with a per-cell noise factor drawn at birth. At division the first daughter inherits a volume fraction $f\sim\mathcal N(0.5,\sigma^2)$ (clipped to $[0.05,0.95]$) and each molecule is assigned to it independently with probability $f$ (binomial partitioning); a beta-binomial option models over-dispersed partitioning. Promoter groups are inherited by both daughters when unreplicated and split one copy per daughter, in random assignment, when replicated. Gene replication, if enabled, doubles the promoter counts (and the propensity of reactions flagged as copy-number dependent) once the cell has completed a set fraction of its cycle, and is reset at division. Populations can be started from per-founder states and volumes (for instance a snapshot of a burnt-in constant-size population). Population control is either constant size (every second daughter replaces a uniformly chosen cell, the behaviour of version 1), free growth (all daughters kept, with uniform subsampling above a cap and book-keeping of the true size), logistic (free growth with an additional hazard $\lambda N/K$) or a mother machine (one daughter kept at random at every division, so that each founder gives one statistically independent lineage and the recorded cells follow the single-lineage rather than the population snapshot distribution).

## Drug and perturbation layer

A perturbation combines a dose schedule $d(t)$ (constant, pulsed, piecewise, one-compartment bolus pharmacokinetics or an arbitrary function) with effects evaluated for every cell at every step: a death hazard
$$h = h_{\max}\frac{d^m}{\mathrm{EC}_{50}^m + d^m}\cdot\frac{K^q}{K^q + c_P^{\,q}},$$
where $c_P$ is the concentration of a protective species (survival probability over a step is $e^{-h\,\Delta t}$); growth inhibition by $1/(1+(d/\mathrm{IC}_{50})^{m_g})$, whose Hill coefficient $m_g$ is separate from the death coefficient $m$ and is 2 in every simulation reported here; a dose-independent fitness cost of a cell state, $1 - c_{\max}\,c_P^{\,q}/(K^q + c_P^{\,q})$, on the growth rate; multiplicative modulation of any parameter by a function of the dose; and genetic perturbations (knockdown, knockout, over-expression) of any parameter in a random fraction of cells, the flag being inherited by daughters.

Two further effects couple the drug to the cell cycle and to the protein that resists it. A cell-cycle-dependent hazard multiplies $h$ by
$$\beta + (1-\beta)\exp\!\left[-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\varphi-\varphi_0}{w}\right)^{2}\right],$$
where $\varphi$ is the fraction of the cell's volume increment completed, $(V-V_{\mathrm{birth}})/(V_{\mathrm{div}}-V_{\mathrm{birth}})$ under a sizer and $(V-V_{\mathrm{birth}})/\Delta$ under an adder, and the fraction of the interdivision time elapsed under a timer (0 at birth, 1 at division), so that killing peaks at $\varphi_0$ and falls to a fraction $\beta$ of the peak elsewhere; $\beta = 1$ is the cycle-independent hazard and $\beta = 0$ confines killing to a window of width $w$ around $\varphi_0$. This represents an agent whose lesions are converted into death during replication. Stoichiometric consumption of a protective protein removes molecules of it at rate
$$k\,d\,V\,\frac{c_P}{c_P + K_m},$$
by a Poisson draw over the step. Because lesions form in proportion to the dose, the rate is set by lesion formation alone while the protein is abundant, so the pool follows the cumulative exposure rather than the peak concentration, and falls with what remains once the pool runs low; resynthesis is whatever the reaction model provides. This is the behaviour of a suicide enzyme such as MGMT, which is inactivated by the very lesion it repairs.

## Lineage statistics

The lineage table records, for every cell, its parent, founder (clone), generation, birth and end times, fate (divided, died, removed, alive) and its volume and state at birth and at the end of its life. From it we compute Pearson correlations of expression at division between mothers and daughters, sisters and cousins; the lineage autocorrelation $r_g$ between a cell and its ancestor $g$ generations earlier, and the memory timescale $\tau$ from $r_g = e^{-g/\tau}$; single-lineage traces by following the ancestral line of a surviving cell; the coefficient of variation squared of concentration across the population versus along single lineages; Luria-Delbrück fluctuation tests (variance of a phenotype across clones grown from single cells compared with the binomial expectation); and MemorySeq-style clonal scores [@shaffer2020], the variance of clone-mean expression divided by its permutation-null expectation.

## Observation models

True counts are converted to observations by a scRNA-seq model in which each cell captures every molecule independently with a Beta-distributed efficiency (mean and coefficient of variation set by the protocol), optional multinomial down-sampling to a Poisson-distributed depth, optional additional dropout and batch scaling; an smFISH model with a fixed detection efficiency; and a time-lapse model that samples a species along lineages at a fixed interval with additive or multiplicative measurement noise. Because counts scale with volume, library size correlates with cell size, reproducing the transcriptome-size confound of real data. Outputs are written as CSV, as AnnData-compatible HDF5 files, or as Newick lineage trees.

## Inference

Snapshot counts of a gene in non-dividing cells follow the Beta-Poisson law of the telegraph model [@peccoud1995], which we evaluate through Kummer's confluent hypergeometric function with rescaled series summation, and maximise by a grid-initialised Nelder-Mead search in log-parameter space. For any model, including populations, we implement sequential Monte Carlo approximate Bayesian computation [@toni2009] with an adaptive tolerance schedule (the $\alpha$ quantile of the previous generation's distances), Gaussian perturbation kernels of variance twice the weighted population variance, importance weights against the prior, and parallel evaluation of the user-supplied distance function; for count distributions we use the Wasserstein-1 distance between simulated and observed samples [@bernton2019].

## Calibration to laboratory data and validation strategy

Reference values were transcribed from the text and tables of the cited articles into `paper/data/` with their provenance (Supplementary Table 4). For the cisplatin case study the model is a resistance gene with slow promoter switching whose protein product protects against a dose-dependent death hazard and against a dose-dependent growth arrest (Methods above), with a 24-h cell cycle (at the fast end of reported U2OS doubling times and consistent with the intermitotic times tracked by @iyer2025). Its six free parameters (activation and inactivation rates, maximal death rate, EC$_{50}$ and Hill coefficient of death, IC$_{50}$ of growth arrest) were fitted by the method of simulated moments: the root-mean-square difference between simulated and observed fractions of cells that died, divided or survived without dividing during 72 h of drug, for the training concentrations (7 and 13 µM), plus a weak penalty keeping the memory of the resistant state near the at least two to three generations inferred from lineage correlations in HCT116 cells, a different cell line from the U2OS cells whose fate fractions are fitted here, which is why the later comparison with those correlations is a consistency check and not an independent test, was minimised with the bounded optimiser described below using common random numbers (the same seeds for every candidate). Validation used only quantities not fitted, but they are not all of one kind. The fate fractions at the held-out concentration (10 µM) and the invariance of single-cell division and death times across concentrations are U2OS measurements, as the training fractions are, and are held out in the ordinary sense. The biphasic kill curve with its plateau and the extent of lineage correlations in fate (first, second and third cousins) are HCT116 measurements, because only that dataset tracked full lineages and only it shows a biphasic decay; these two are therefore compared across cell lines, and the lineage correlations additionally set the memory prior, so neither is an independent test of the calibrated model. The protocol is deliberately generic: any observable computable from a `PopulationResult` (kill curves, fate fractions, lineage correlations, snapshot distributions) can enter the distance, and the split between training and validation conditions is declared before fitting.

## Clinical regimens, treatment outcomes and schedule optimisation

Regimens are built from the schedule primitives of the perturbation layer: `PulsedDose` with a finite number of cycles, `PiecewiseDose` for arbitrary calendars (the S1320 schedule), `daily_boluses` and `cycle_days` for daily oral doses with one-compartment pharmacokinetics, and `AdaptiveDose`, a feedback schedule that withdraws the drug when the population falls below a fraction of its initial size and resumes it when the population regrows above another fraction, evaluated with the current population size at every step [@zhang2017adaptive]. Outcomes computed from a simulation are the net growth rate, the depth of response (log$_{10}$ of the ratio of the initial to the smallest population), the time to progression (first time the population exceeds a threshold multiple of its running minimum, the analogue of RECIST progression from the nadir; the multiple is an argument of the function, 1.2 by default, and the melanoma case study below sets it to 1.73; censored at the end of the simulation), the extinction probability over replicates and the cumulative dose actually applied. `optimize_schedule` minimises any such outcome over a box of schedule parameters: a log-spaced grid (or a Latin hypercube when the grid is large) is evaluated with the same random seeds for every candidate, and the best point is refined by Nelder-Mead in a bounded transform of the box; constraints such as a fixed cumulative dose enter as penalties or as the parameterisation (for instance a per-day dose equal to the budget divided by the number of dosing days). The case studies are run at the scale of a tumour rather than a culture: the cell cycle of the model is the net doubling time of the tumour (4 weeks for melanoma, 40 days for glioblastoma). The glioblastoma value lies within the volume doubling times measured for untreated tumours, a median of 21 days in one magnetic-resonance series and 50 days in another [@ellingson2016; @stensjoen2015]; the melanoma value is an assumption, since net doubling times of treated cutaneous melanoma are not established, and it sets the time axis of the melanoma case study rather than the ranking of schedules, which follows from the resistance mechanism. Growth is exponential throughout, which is a simplification: the same measurements favour Gompertzian slowing in large glioblastomas [@stensjoen2015], so absolute regrowth times in Fig. 8 are shorter than a saturating model would give. Gene-expression rates are scaled to the hour (mRNA half-life 7 h and protein half-life 35 h, generic mammalian values; at the tumour time scale both species turn over many times per generation, so the results are insensitive to them and all heritable memory resides in the promoter), and founders are drawn from the stationary distribution of promoter states. The melanoma case uses a pre-resistant fraction of 1:200 (a per-marker frequency [@shaffer2017] used as a proxy for the functional pre-resistant fraction, which is not the same quantity, and 5- to 50-fold higher than the 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000 at which drug-naive precursors of resistant fates have been traced [@emert2021]; the arms of Fig. 8 that lose control therefore do so earlier than the traced frequency would give, while those censored at 52 weeks are unaffected), with a memory of five net doublings (the model's generation is the net doubling of the tumour, a modelling choice rather than the cell-division-counted memory of memory sequencing [@shaffer2020]) and drug effects at the fractional-killing dose: sensitive cells arrest and die with a half-life of two weeks, cells in the resistant state are protected, and the drug stabilises the resistant state ten-fold (the drug-induced reprogramming of @shaffer2017). Three variants of the resistant state are compared: full protection without cost; a 50% fitness cost in the absence of drug [@dasthakur2013]; and partial protection, in which resistant cells grow at half speed under drug. Progression is scored in two ways: from the nadir, as in RECIST (a 20% increase in diameter, taken as a 1.73-fold increase in cell number, after the 8-week lead-in; a well-mixed cell count cannot represent RECIST's absolute 5 mm threshold, its new-lesion and non-target criteria or its sum-of-diameters basis, and it assumes cell number proportional to volume, so this is an analogue rather than the clinical end point), and as loss of control, the time after randomisation at which the population exceeds 120% of its pre-treatment size, the end point of adaptive-therapy studies, which is the one used to compare schedules that deliberately let the tumour regrow. The two differ in their reference point as well as in their multiple: progression from the nadir is 1.73 times the running minimum of the population after randomisation, whereas loss of control is 1.2 times its size before treatment, which does not depend on how deep the response was. Both are computed for every arm of the melanoma study and both are reported. The temozolomide case uses a one-compartment elimination half-life of 2.1 h [@ostermann2004] (a plasma value from a three-compartment population model of plasma and cerebrospinal fluid; that study measured cerebrospinal fluid rather than tumour tissue and found its exposure to be 20% of plasma exposure, so a plasma half-life stands here as a surrogate for what the tumour sees), the regimens of RTOG 0525 [@gilbert2013] (200 mg/m² on days 1-5 or 100 mg/m² on days 1-21 of 28-day cycles, six cycles), MGMT-expressing fractions of 1% and 30% for methylated and unmethylated promoters (illustrative values bracketing low and high expression rather than measured fractions; methylation shifts graded expression rather than a bimodal positive fraction), and MGMT either stable or consumed stoichiometrically by the drug, one molecule per lesion repaired ($k = 300$, $K_m = 150$ in the expression above). The rate constant was chosen so that the consumption rate at the standard bolus peak equals that of a dose-scaled first-order degradation, which isolates the consequence of letting depletion follow the cumulative exposure instead of the peak concentration; this is the suicide-enzyme behaviour that motivated dose-dense schedules.

## Random regulatory networks

Random networks are built with a chosen number of activating and inhibiting edges under Erdős-Rényi or preferential-attachment (scale-free) sampling; regulation acts on the transcription rate of constitutive genes or on the promoter activation rate of telegraph genes through AND-combined Hill functions; per-gene rate constants can be drawn log-uniformly from ranges.

## Validation protocols

Exactness was tested by Kolmogorov-Smirnov distances between simulated stationary distributions and the analytic laws (Poisson for birth-death, Beta-Poisson for the telegraph model, negative binomial for bursty protein production [@friedman2006; @shahrezaei2008]), by agreement between kernels, and by a two-sample comparison against JumpProcesses.jl [@sciml] on the same model.

The population and lineage layers were then validated against exact solutions for growing, dividing populations, which exist for this class of model and take different forms for a single lineage and for a population snapshot [@beentjes2020; @jia2023; @thomas2017; @thomas2021]. Four analytically solvable members of that class were simulated in both modes: constitutive production, production in bursts of fixed size, and a two-state promoter, each with a memoryless interdivision time; and volume-scaled synthesis from a gene that replicates at mid-cycle, with a deterministic cycle and exponential volume growth. Single lineages were generated with a mother-machine population control that keeps one daughter at random at every division, so that the recorded cells are independent; population snapshots were generated by free branching growth with uniform subsampling, which preserves the snapshot distribution. For the memoryless models the exact stationary law of the scheme the simulator runs is the leading eigenvector of one update step, exact kinetics over $dt$ followed by division with probability $1 - \mathrm{e}^{-dt/T}$, with the division operator carrying a factor of two in the population mode; it was computed by matrix exponentiation and checked against the closed-form factorial moments, and the step size was scanned to show the discretisation of the interdivision time vanishing. For the replication model the count distribution at every cycle phase is exactly Poisson, and the cycle-phase weights of the population were obtained by iterating the age map of the scheme from the founder ages used, so that no assumption about convergence to a stable age distribution enters. Each simulated distribution was compared with the exact law of its own mode and with that of the other mode, the second as a check that the comparison can tell them apart (Supplementary Note 14). Population-level properties were compared with the analytic expectations for cell-size scaling and concentration homeostasis [@bertaux2018], partitioning noise [@huh2011], lineage versus population statistics [@zhang2025] and cell-cycle-dependent burst parameters [@sukys2025]. Persister simulations were compared with the quantitative signatures reported by @iyer2025, @corigliano2025 and @harmange2023, and with the phenotypic-selection model of @lasri2020. All scripts, seeds and outputs are in the `paper/` directory of the repository.

## Identifiability and numerical robustness of the calibration

Because six parameters were fitted to six measured fractions, the calibration was examined in three ways. A conditional profile moves one parameter at a time over its full range on a log grid of eleven points, holding the others at their fitted values, and records the root-mean-square error of the fate fractions without the memory prior; parameters whose error stays within the binomial standard error of the measurement over a wide range are not determined by these data. A two-dimensional slice does the same over the memory of the resistant state and the fraction of cells in it, the two quantities that trade off most directly, with the death parameters held at their fitted values; a slice was used in preference to a profile with re-optimisation because, at this cost per evaluation, the inner search did not reliably recover the fitted solution and reported spuriously poor fits at short memory. Numerical robustness was checked by halving the integration step and comparing the simulated fractions with the seed-to-seed spread at the fitted parameters.

Because the lethality of cisplatin is enhanced during replication, the position of a cell in its division cycle when the drug arrives is a competing explanation for the dose-invariance of death timing and for the fraction of cells that survive without dividing. To test it, the same six parameters were refitted together with a seventh, the cycle-independent fraction $\beta$ of the death hazard, with the peak of killing half-way through the volume added over the cycle ($\varphi_0 = 0.5$, $w = 0.15$), under the same objective, bounds, optimiser budget and seeds as the main fit. The two fits are compared by the root-mean-square error of the fate fractions without the memory prior, by their predictions at the held-out concentration, and by a conditional profile over $\beta$ with the remaining parameters held at the cycle-dependent fit. The same gate ($\beta = 0.25$, $\varphi_0 = 0.5$, $w = 0.15$) was then applied to the persister simulations of Fig. 4 and the melanoma simulations of Fig. 8a-e, everything else unchanged, to test whether their conclusions depend on the hazard being cycle-blind (Supplementary Note 13). The window position is a modelling choice rather than a claim about either agent: what is tested is robustness to a strong cell-cycle gate of any kind.

## Statistics and reproducibility

No statistical method was used to predetermine sample size, and no experimental data were generated in this study; sample sizes are the numbers of simulated cells, founders or seeds stated in each figure legend and in Supplementary Table 2. No data were excluded. Randomisation and blinding do not apply: every result is a deterministic function of an explicit random seed, and all seeds are fixed in the scripts that generate the figures. Replicates are independent simulations differing only in seed; where a mean and a standard deviation are reported, the number of seeds is given in the legend. Kolmogorov-Smirnov distances are compared with the 99% critical value for the stated sample size. Distributional agreement between kernels and with an independent implementation was assessed by two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics. Correlations are Pearson correlations unless stated otherwise; correlations of binary fates between related cells are phi coefficients. Posterior summaries are medians, because the posteriors are asymmetric on the log scale. Schedules within a scan are evaluated on common random numbers, the same seeds for every point of the grid, so they are compared by the paired differences over those seeds rather than by an unpaired standard error, which the shared founder population would make invalid; a margin is called resolved when a two-sided paired Student's t-test rejects equality at the 5% level, which on four degrees of freedom requires the margin to exceed 2.776 of its paired standard errors rather than two. Multithreaded runs give results identical to single-threaded runs, because each cell carries its own random number generator seeded from its parent.
