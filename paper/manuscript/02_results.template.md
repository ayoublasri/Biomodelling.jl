# Results

## A layered framework for stochastic simulation in growing, dividing and drug-treated populations

Biomodelling.jl 2.0 is organised in layers (Figure 1). A model is a list of reactions with mass-action, Hill or custom kinetics and named parameters; promoter states are explicit species grouped so that they are inherited at division and doubled at replication. Four kernels advance a cell's state at fixed volume: the exact direct method, fixed-step tau-leaping, a hybrid scheme that falls back to the exact method whenever a leap would produce a negative count, and adaptive tau-leaping with critical-reaction handling. The population layer grows each cell's volume exponentially, scales propensities with volume so that concentrations are invariant, replicates genes at a set point of the cycle, divides cells by a sizer, adder or timer rule with binomial (or over-dispersed) partitioning of molecules, and controls the population size (constant size by Moran-like replacement, free growth, or logistic). A drug and perturbation layer applies dose schedules, state-dependent death, growth inhibition, drug-induced rate changes and genetic perturbations. Every cell's ancestry, fate and state at birth and division are recorded in a lineage table, from which heritability, autocorrelation, memory timescales, fluctuation-test and clonal-memory statistics are computed. Observation models convert true counts into scRNA-seq, smFISH or time-lapse measurements, and the same forward model drives likelihood-free inference. ## The engine is exact and agrees with an independent implementation

We first verified the kernels against exact stationary laws (Figure 2). For a birth-death process the simulated distribution of 50 000 cells is indistinguishable from the Poisson law (Figure 2a); for the telegraph model in an intermediate and in a bursty regime it matches the Beta-Poisson distribution of @peccoud1995 (Figure 2b); and for bursty protein production it matches the negative binomial (Figure 2c). Across kernels, Kolmogorov-Smirnov distances to the exact law with 20 000 cells are below the 99% critical value for the direct method and for the hybrid and adaptive schemes at their default tolerances, at wall-clock costs of {{fig2d_times}} per 10⁴ cells (Figure 2d). Simulating the same telegraph model with JumpProcesses.jl gives distributions whose two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic against Biomodelling.jl is {{ks_two}} (99% critical value {{ks_crit}}; Figure 2e), with a {{speed_ratio}} difference in serial wall-clock time. Population simulations scale linearly with the number of cells and approximately linearly with the number of reactions: {{runtime_10k}} for {{runtime_case}} with four threads (Figure 2f).

## Growth, division and partitioning generate expression memory, size scaling and cell-cycle effects

The physiology of the population layer is sufficient to produce the properties of single-cell data that phenomenological simulators must impose by hand (Figure 3). In a single lineage of a slowly switching gene, mRNA and protein track volume within a cycle and are halved at division, while the promoter state, inherited by both daughters, persists over many divisions (Figure 3a). For a fast-switching gene without replication, transcript numbers scale linearly with cell volume so that concentration is homeostatic; adding mid-cycle gene replication doubles transcription in the second half of the cycle and lifts counts of large cells above the volume-scaling line (Figure 3b), the copy-number effect that mechanistic fits to scRNA-seq data must account for [@sukys2025]. Correlations of protein concentration at division between mothers and daughters, sisters and cousins decrease from {{md_slow}} to {{md_fast}} as the promoter switching rate crosses the inverse cell-cycle time (Figure 3c), with sisters more correlated than mother-daughter pairs and cousins less, and mRNA less heritable than protein. The memory timescale extracted from the generation-resolved lineage autocorrelation is set by the promoter switching time and is nearly independent of the cell-cycle time as long as memory outlasts the cycle; when the two are comparable the estimate is bounded below by one cell cycle, the resolution of the autocorrelation (Figure 3d). Following @zhang2025, the coefficient of variation of a stable protein's concentration is larger across the population than along single lineages, whether or not cell-cycle times are noisy, and over-dispersed partitioning adds variability in both perspectives (Figure 3e). Finally, telegraph parameters fitted separately to cells before and after replication differ by the expected factor of two in burst frequency, and a fit that ignores the cell cycle returns intermediate values (Figure 3f).

## A single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces persister biology

To test whether heritable expression states suffice to reproduce the observed phenomenology of drug-tolerant persisters, we coupled a resistance gene with slow promoter switching (memory timescale {{mem_time}} time units, cell-cycle time 20) to a death hazard that acts on the drug dose and is suppressed by the concentration of the gene's protein product (Methods). Under continuous, pulsed and reduced-dose schedules the population first collapses as low-expressing cells die and then regrows from the high-expressing survivors (Figure 4a); kill curves are biphasic with a surviving fraction that decreases with dose (Figure 4b). The net rate of population decline over the first 30 time units of treatment rises from {{decay_lo}} to {{decay_hi}} per time unit between doses 0.5 and 2 ({{decay_fold}}-fold), while the mean division time of cells born under drug changes by only {{divtime_change}} and the mean time to death of killed cells only moves from {{deathtime_range}} time units (Figure 4c), reproducing the observation of @iyer2025 that population decay depends strongly on dose even though single-cell division and death times barely do, because fate is decided by cell states present before the drug. Accordingly, related cells born in the 25 time units before the drug share the fate of their lineages (extinction, or survival of at least one descendant): a cell whose sister founded a surviving lineage does so itself with probability {{sis_cond}}, against a marginal probability of {{sis_marg}} ({{sis_fold}}-fold enrichment over {{sis_n}} sister pairs; {{cous_fold}}-fold for cousins), whereas in the fast-switching control, in which survival is rarer, the conditional and marginal probabilities coincide ({{sis_cond_fast}} versus {{sis_marg_fast}}; Figure 4d). When tolerance pre-exists, survivors descend from a minority of founder clones and the effective number of clones drops from {{clones_before}} to {{clones_after_pre}}, whereas when tolerance is drug-induced (the drug raises the promoter activation rate of every cell) barcode diversity is largely preserved ({{clones_after_ind}}; Figure 4e), the discriminating signature used in barcoding experiments [@shaffer2017; @iyer2025]. Disrupting expression memory changes this outcome: accelerating promoter switching 20-fold from two cell-cycle times before the drug, as memory-disrupting epigenetic compounds do [@harmange2023], leaves the number of surviving clones unchanged when the acceleration stops at the moment the drug arrives ({{clones_none}} versus {{clones_predrug}} clones), because it reshuffles which cells are in the resistant state without changing their number, but reduces it to {{clones_during}} clones ({{cells_during}} cells, against {{cells_none}}) when the acceleration continues during exposure (Figure 4f), because resistant cells now leave the protected state within a fraction of the time they need to establish a colony: in the model, memory must be disrupted while the drug is present. A transient drug pulse enriches high-expressing cells and, with slow switching, the enrichment persists for many generations after the drug is removed, whereas with fast switching it relaxes within a few cycles (Figure 4g): the phenotypic selection with stable inheritance of the original MGMT model [@lasri2020]. Finally, scanning release periods and doses of intermittent schedules (Figure 4h; net growth rate of the population over a 200-time-unit course), the most effective schedule is {{best_pre}} for pre-existing tolerance, {{best_cost}} when the resistant state carries a fitness cost, and {{best_ind}} for drug-induced tolerance (the drug raising the promoter activation rate 100-fold at dose 1). Without a fitness cost, therefore, nothing beats continuous dosing at the highest dose and every release period lets the resistant subpopulation regrow. With a fitness cost of the resistant state the population declines under every schedule with dose ≥ 0.5, and release periods of up to 10 time units are as effective as continuous dosing ({{cont_cost}} per time unit under continuous dose 2), because cells that leave the resistant state during the holiday are killed on re-exposure. For drug-induced tolerance the ranking of doses reverses: the highest dose induces tolerance in more cells than it kills, so dose 1 outperforms dose 2 at every release period ({{best_ind}}, against {{cont_ind}} for continuous dose 2), the intermediate-dose optimum reported by @corigliano2025; the benefit of release periods reported in that work does not appear in this model, in which the induced state decays at the same rate with and without drug, so that a holiday only postpones the death of reverting cells while all cells grow.

## Ground truth for memory genes, network inference, imputation and perturbation prediction

Because every cell's clone, lineage and true state are known, the framework supplies ground truth that observational data cannot (Figure 5). For 40 independent genes whose memory times span three decades, MemorySeq-style clonal variance scores computed on true concentrations rise from {{score_min}} for genes with memory times far below the cell-cycle time to {{score_max}} for the slowest genes, and the ranking is preserved when scores are computed on sequenced counts at 20% capture efficiency (Figure 5a); the score separates genes with memory times above the cycle time from the rest with an area under the receiver operating curve of {{auc_true}} (true) and {{auc_seq}} (sequenced). For a 30-gene network simulated at fixed volume, in a dividing population and after sequencing, correlation-based and GENIE3 inference lose most of their accuracy in the dividing population when raw counts are used (area under the precision-recall curve {{aupr_pearson_fixed}} to {{aupr_pearson_counts}} for Pearson correlation and {{aupr_genie3_fixed}} to {{aupr_genie3_counts}} for GENIE3, against {{aupr_random}} for a random ranking), because volume and gene copy number induce positive correlations between all genes. Normalising counts to concentrations removes the volume effect but recovers little ({{aupr_pearson_conc}} and {{aupr_genie3_conc}}), since the dependence of expression on copy number and cell age remains, whereas regressing expression on the true cell-cycle covariates (copy number and age, which the simulation provides and which cell-cycle scoring approximates in real data) before inference recovers most of the loss ({{aupr_pearson_reg}} and {{aupr_genie3_reg}}). Sequencing at 15% capture efficiency lowers accuracy further ({{aupr_pearson_seq}} for raw and {{aupr_pearson_norm}} for library-size-normalised counts with Pearson correlation; {{aupr_genie3_seq}} and {{aupr_genie3_norm}} with GENIE3; Figure 5b). Imputation with kNN-smoothing and MAGIC before inference changes the area under the precision-recall curve by {{imp_effect}} (Figure 5c), consistent with the conclusions of the original benchmark [@lasri2022]. Finally, knocking down the five most connected regulators of a 20-gene network produces true fold changes that a no-change baseline and the propagation of observational correlations predict with coefficients of determination of {{r2_zero}} and {{r2_corr}} respectively (Figure 5d), illustrating how causal, out-of-distribution ground truth can be generated for the evaluation of perturbation-prediction models [@ahlmanneltze2025; @vollenweider2026].

## The forward model supports inference and quantifies the bias of ignoring division

On a snapshot of 2 000 non-dividing cells, ABC-SMC with log-uniform priors over three decades and the Wasserstein distance between simulated and observed counts [@bernton2019] concentrates on the true telegraph parameters and agrees with the exact maximum-likelihood estimate (Figure 6a; posterior medians {{abc_a}} for truth (0.3, 0.6, 20)), with the broadest posterior on k_off, the parameter that a single snapshot constrains least. On a snapshot of a growing and dividing population with volume-scaled transcription and mid-cycle replication, the naive telegraph fit is biased ({{naive_b}}) because volume and copy number inflate counts and their dispersion, whereas ABC with the population simulator as forward model recovers the truth ({{abc_b}}; Figure 6b). Drug parameters can be inferred from population data in the same way: from a kill curve and the concordance of sister fates, the posterior over the maximal death rate and the protection threshold is a ridge (weighted correlation of log h_max and log K of {{ridge_corr}}) on which the truth (0.5, 150) lies (Figure 6c), and posterior predictive kill curves simulated at the posterior median reproduce the observed one (Figure 6d): the two summaries constrain the product of death rate and unprotected fraction, and separating the two parameters requires a measurement of the protective protein, an example of the experimental-design questions the forward model can answer before an experiment is run.

## The persister model calibrates to laboratory time-lapse data and predicts held-out conditions

A simulator is only useful for treatment questions if it can be fitted to the data a laboratory actually produces and then predicts what it was not fitted to. We therefore calibrated the resistance-gene model to the time-lapse measurements of @iyer2025, who tracked U2OS cells for three days under 7, 10 and 13 µM cisplatin and recorded, for every cell present at drug addition, whether it died, divided or survived without dividing (Table S3). Six parameters (promoter switching rates, maximal death rate, EC$_{50}$ and Hill coefficient of death, IC$_{50}$ of growth arrest) were fitted to the fate fractions at 7 and 13 µM by simulated moments with common random numbers, with a weak prior keeping the memory of the resistant state near the two to three generations that the same study inferred from lineage correlations (Methods). The calibrated model (Figure 7f; memory {{cal_memgen}} generations, resistant fraction {{cal_pon}}, EC$_{50}$ {{cal_ec50}} µM) reproduces the training fractions with a root-mean-square error of {{rmse_train}} and predicts the held-out 10 µM condition with an error of {{rmse_heldout}} (observed died, divided and survived fractions {{obs_10}}; predicted {{pred_10}}; Figure 7a). Three properties that were not fitted are also reproduced. The predicted kill curve at the concentration that kills 64% of cells, the fraction observed in HCT116 cells, is biphasic, with a plateau of surviving resistant cells emerging after {{plateau_days}} days (Figure 7b), as in the HCT116 experiment. Death fates are correlated between sisters ({{phi_sis}}), first cousins ({{phi_c1}}) and second cousins ({{phi_c2}}) but not between third cousins ({{phi_c3}}) or unrelated cells ({{phi_unrel}}; Figure 7c), the pattern reported for HCT116 lineages. And the mean division time of cells born under drug and the mean time to death change by only {{divtime_shift}} and {{deathtime_shift}} between 7 and 13 µM while the death fraction rises from {{obs_died_7}} to {{obs_died_13}} (Figure 7d), the dose-invariance of single-cell timing that motivated the original study. The calibration landscape (Figure 7e) shows how the fitted death rate and EC$_{50}$ are constrained by the fate fractions alone; the same protocol, with the same functions, applies to any observable that a laboratory records.

## Dose and schedule optimisation against clinical regimens

With a calibrated model the framework becomes a schedule testbed. We first asked which dosing schedule minimises regrowth for a melanoma-like population with a rare, heritable pre-resistant state [@shaffer2017] treated at a fractional-killing dose, comparing continuous dosing with the intermittent schedule of the SWOG S1320 trial, in which patients received a continuous 8-week lead-in and were then randomised to continuous dosing or to cycles of 3 weeks off and 5 weeks on [@algazi2020], and with adaptive therapy, in which the drug is withdrawn when the population has shrunk to half its initial size and resumed when it regrows to the initial size [@zhang2017adaptive]. When the resistant state carries no fitness cost, as the lineage data of @iyer2025 indicate for pre-existing tolerance, continuous dosing gives the longest time to progression ({{ttp_cont_nocost}} weeks against {{ttp_int_nocost}} for the intermittent schedule and {{ttp_adapt_nocost}} for adaptive therapy; Figure 8a,c), the ranking observed in the trial, where continuous dosing gave a median progression-free survival of 9.0 months against 5.5 months for intermittent dosing. When resistant cells pay a fitness cost in the absence of drug, the drug addiction that made intermittent dosing superior in patient-derived xenografts [@dasthakur2013], the ranking reverses: the intermittent schedule and adaptive therapy delay progression to {{ttp_int_cost}} and {{ttp_adapt_cost}} weeks against {{ttp_cont_cost}} for continuous dosing, with adaptive therapy using {{dose_adapt_cost}} of the cumulative dose (Figure 8b,c). The optimiser makes the dependence explicit: over intermittent schedules of any period and duty cycle, the best schedule without a fitness cost is {{best_nocost}}, and with a fitness cost {{best_cost}} (Figure 8d). The model therefore reproduces both clinical and preclinical outcomes and attributes their difference to a measurable property of the resistant cells, their fitness without drug, which the framework can estimate from growth data before a schedule is chosen.

The second case study concerns glioblastoma, where the RTOG 0525 trial compared the standard temozolomide regimen (150-200 mg/m² on days 1-5 of 28-day cycles) with a dose-dense regimen (75-100 mg/m² on days 1-21) that delivers about twice the cumulative dose, and found no difference in overall survival (16.6 against 14.9 months) for either MGMT-methylated or unmethylated tumours [@gilbert2013]. Simulating both regimens with one-compartment pharmacokinetics (elimination half-life 2.1 h; @ostermann2004) on populations in which 1% (methylated) or 30% (unmethylated) of cells express MGMT, with MGMT protein protecting cells from the drug (the phenotypic-selection model of @lasri2020), {{gbm_summary}} (Figure 8e,f). At equal cumulative dose the number of dosing days per cycle {{gbm_days_summary}} (Figure 8g). These simulations are not fits to the trials, whose end points depend on much that the model omits, but they show how the framework turns a proposed regimen into a quantitative, mechanism-resolved prediction, and how it identifies which biological measurement, the fitness of resistant cells without drug or the fraction of cells protected, decides between regimens.
