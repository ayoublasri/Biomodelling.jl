# Results

## A layered framework for stochastic simulation in growing, dividing and drug-treated populations

Biomodelling.jl 2.0 is organised in layers (Figure 1). A model is a list of reactions with mass-action, Hill or custom kinetics and named parameters; promoter states are explicit species grouped so that they are inherited at division and doubled at replication. Four kernels advance a cell's state at fixed volume: the exact direct method, fixed-step tau-leaping, a hybrid scheme that falls back to the exact method whenever a leap would produce a negative count, and adaptive tau-leaping with critical-reaction handling. The population layer grows each cell's volume exponentially, scales propensities with volume so that concentrations are invariant, replicates genes at a set point of the cycle, divides cells by a sizer, adder or timer rule with binomial (or over-dispersed) partitioning of molecules, and controls the population size (constant size by Moran-like replacement, free growth, or logistic). A drug and perturbation layer applies dose schedules, state-dependent death, growth inhibition, drug-induced rate changes and genetic perturbations. Every cell's ancestry, fate and state at birth and division are recorded in a lineage table, from which heritability, autocorrelation, memory timescales, fluctuation-test and clonal-memory statistics are computed. Observation models convert true counts into scRNA-seq, smFISH or time-lapse measurements, and the same forward model drives likelihood-free inference. Table 1 compares these capabilities with those of the simulators most used to benchmark single-cell methods.

## The engine is exact and agrees with an independent implementation

We first verified the kernels against exact stationary laws (Figure 2). For a birth-death process the simulated distribution of 50 000 cells is indistinguishable from the Poisson law (Figure 2a); for the telegraph model in an intermediate and in a bursty regime it matches the Beta-Poisson distribution of @peccoud1995 (Figure 2b); and for bursty protein production it matches the negative binomial (Figure 2c). Across kernels, Kolmogorov-Smirnov distances to the exact law with 20 000 cells are below the 99% critical value for the direct method and for the hybrid and adaptive schemes at their default tolerances, at wall-clock costs of {{fig2d_times}} per 10⁴ cells (Figure 2d). Simulating the same telegraph model with JumpProcesses.jl gives distributions whose two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic against Biomodelling.jl is {{ks_two}} (99% critical value {{ks_crit}}; Figure 2e), with a {{speed_ratio}} difference in serial wall-clock time. Population simulations scale linearly with the number of cells and approximately linearly with the number of reactions: {{runtime_10k}} for {{runtime_case}} with four threads (Figure 2f).

## Growth, division and partitioning generate expression memory, size scaling and cell-cycle effects

The physiology of the population layer is sufficient to produce the properties of single-cell data that phenomenological simulators must impose by hand (Figure 3). In a single lineage of a slowly switching gene, mRNA and protein track volume within a cycle and are halved at division, while the promoter state, inherited by both daughters, persists over many divisions (Figure 3a). For a fast-switching gene without replication, transcript numbers scale linearly with cell volume so that concentration is homeostatic; adding mid-cycle gene replication doubles transcription in the second half of the cycle and lifts counts of large cells above the volume-scaling line (Figure 3b), the copy-number effect that mechanistic fits to scRNA-seq data must account for [@sukys2025]. Correlations of protein concentration at division between mothers and daughters, sisters and cousins decrease from {{md_slow}} to {{md_fast}} as the promoter switching rate crosses the inverse cell-cycle time (Figure 3c), with sisters more correlated than mother-daughter pairs and cousins less, and mRNA less heritable than protein. The memory timescale extracted from the lineage autocorrelation is set by the promoter switching time and is nearly independent of the cell-cycle time (Figure 3d). Following @zhang2025, the coefficient of variation of a stable protein's concentration is larger across the population than along single lineages when cell-cycle times are noisy, and partitioning noise adds variability in both perspectives (Figure 3e). Finally, telegraph parameters fitted separately to cells before and after replication differ by the expected factor of two in burst frequency, and a fit that ignores the cell cycle returns intermediate values (Figure 3f).

## A single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces persister biology

To test whether heritable expression states suffice to reproduce the observed phenomenology of drug-tolerant persisters, we coupled a resistance gene with slow promoter switching (memory timescale {{mem_time}} time units, cell-cycle time 20) to a death hazard that acts on the drug dose and is suppressed by the concentration of the gene's protein product (Methods). Under continuous, pulsed and reduced-dose schedules the population first collapses as low-expressing cells die and then regrows from the high-expressing survivors (Figure 4a); kill curves are biphasic with a surviving fraction that decreases with dose (Figure 4b). The population decay rate over the first 30 time units of treatment increases {{decay_fold}}-fold between the lowest and highest dose, while the mean division time of cells born under drug changes by only {{divtime_change}} (Figure 4c), reproducing the observation of @iyer2025 that population decay depends strongly on dose even though single-cell division and death times do not, because fate is decided by cell states present before the drug. Accordingly, sisters and cousins born before the drug share their fate more often than expected for independent cells (concordance {{sis_conc}} versus {{sis_exp}} for sisters in the memory model) whereas a fast-switching control shows no excess concordance (Figure 4d). When tolerance pre-exists, survivors descend from a minority of founder clones and the effective number of clones drops from {{clones_before}} to {{clones_after_pre}}, whereas when tolerance is drug-induced (the drug raises the promoter activation rate) barcode diversity is largely preserved ({{clones_after_ind}}; Figure 4e), the discriminating signature used in barcoding experiments [@shaffer2017; @iyer2025]. Scanning release periods and doses of intermittent schedules shows that, for drug-induced tolerance, the lowest long-term growth rate is reached at a non-zero release period and an intermediate dose, whereas for pre-existing tolerance continuous high dose remains best (Figure 4f), which is the qualitative conclusion of @corigliano2025 for induced persistence. Accelerating promoter switching for two cell-cycle times before the drug, a memory-disrupting pretreatment [@harmange2023], reduces the number of surviving clones from {{clones_np}} to {{clones_p}} (Figure 4g). Finally, a transient drug pulse enriches high-expressing cells and, with slow switching, the enrichment persists for many generations after the drug is removed, whereas with fast switching it relaxes within a few cycles (Figure 4h): the phenotypic selection with stable inheritance of the original MGMT model [@lasri2020].

## Ground truth for memory genes, network inference, imputation and perturbation prediction

Because every cell's clone, lineage and true state are known, the framework supplies ground truth that observational data cannot (Figure 5). For 40 independent genes whose memory times span three decades, MemorySeq-style clonal variance scores computed on true concentrations rise from {{score_min}} for genes with memory times far below the cell-cycle time to {{score_max}} for the slowest genes, and the ranking is preserved when scores are computed on sequenced counts at 20% capture efficiency (Figure 5a); the score separates genes with memory times above the cycle time from the rest with an area under the receiver operating curve of {{auc_true}} (true) and {{auc_seq}} (sequenced). For a 30-gene network simulated at fixed volume, in a dividing population and after sequencing, correlation-based and GENIE3 inference lose accuracy in the dividing population when raw counts are used, because volume and copy number induce correlations between all genes; normalising counts to concentrations (or to library size after sequencing) recovers most of the loss (Figure 5b). Imputation with kNN-smoothing and MAGIC before inference changes the area under the precision-recall curve by {{imp_effect}} (Figure 5c), consistent with the conclusions of the original benchmark [@lasri2022]. Finally, knocking down the five most connected regulators of a 20-gene network produces true fold changes that a no-change baseline and the propagation of observational correlations predict with coefficients of determination of {{r2_zero}} and {{r2_corr}} respectively (Figure 5d), illustrating how causal, out-of-distribution ground truth can be generated for the evaluation of perturbation-prediction models [@ahlmanneltze2025; @vollenweider2026].

## The forward model supports inference and quantifies the bias of ignoring division

On a snapshot of 2 000 non-dividing cells, ABC-SMC with log-uniform priors concentrates on the true telegraph parameters and agrees with the exact maximum-likelihood estimate (Figure 6a; posterior means {{abc_a}} for truth (0.3, 0.6, 20)). On a snapshot of a growing and dividing population with volume-scaled transcription and mid-cycle replication, the naive telegraph fit is biased ({{naive_b}}) because volume and copy number inflate counts and their dispersion, whereas ABC with the population simulator as forward model recovers the truth ({{abc_b}}; Figure 6b). Drug parameters are also identifiable from population data: from a kill curve and the concordance of sister fates, the death rate and the protection threshold are recovered with posterior means {{abc_c}} for truth (0.5, 150) (Figure 6c), and posterior predictive kill curves envelope the observed one (Figure 6d).
