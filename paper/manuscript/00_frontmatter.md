---
title: "Mechanistic simulation of heritable expression states, cell division and drug response in single-cell populations"
author:
  - "Ayoub Lasri^1^"
date: "^1^ Department of Physiology and Medical Physics, Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland, Dublin, Ireland. Correspondence: alasri@berache.com"
abstract: |
  Heritable, non-genetic differences in gene expression decide which cancer cells survive a drug, yet the simulators used to benchmark single-cell methods model neither cell growth nor division, and therefore cannot generate such states from first principles. Here we present Biomodelling.jl 2.0, a framework in which stochastic reaction kinetics run inside growing, dividing and drug-treated cells. Exact, hybrid and adaptive kernels are coupled to exponential growth, volume-scaled transcription, gene replication and division with binomial partitioning and promoter inheritance, to dose schedules and state-dependent killing, to a complete lineage record, to single-cell observation models and to likelihood-free inference. Expression memory, cell-size scaling and cell-cycle-dependent bursting emerge from this physiology rather than being imposed. A single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces the reported signatures of drug-tolerant persisters, and the framework supplies the ground truth that benchmarks of memory-gene detection, network inference and perturbation prediction lack. Calibrated to time-lapse measurements of cisplatin-treated cells, the model predicts held-out concentrations, lineage correlations and single-cell timing. Used with a built-in optimiser, it recovers the opposite rankings of intermittent dosing reported in a melanoma trial and in xenografts, attributes the difference to the fitness of resistant cells without drug, and searches for better schedules.
