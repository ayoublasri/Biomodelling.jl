---
title: "Biomodelling.jl 2.0: mechanistic simulation of heritable gene-expression states, cell division and drug response for single-cell benchmarking and persister biology"
author:
  - "Ayoub Lasri^1^"
  - "Marc Sturrock^1^"
date: "^1^ Department of Physiology and Medical Physics, Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland, Dublin, Ireland"
abstract: |
  Non-genetic heterogeneity, and in particular expression states that are inherited across cell divisions, decides which cancer cells survive drug treatment, yet the simulators used to benchmark single-cell methods model neither cell growth nor division, and therefore cannot generate such states from first principles. We present Biomodelling.jl 2.0, a rewrite of a Julia framework for stochastic simulation of gene regulatory networks inside growing, dividing and drug-treated cell populations. The framework couples exact, hybrid and adaptive stochastic kinetics to exponential growth, volume-scaled transcription, gene replication, division with binomial partitioning and promoter inheritance, and population control; adds dose schedules, state-dependent death, growth inhibition and genetic perturbations; records every cell's lineage; converts molecule counts into scRNA-seq, smFISH and time-lapse observations; and provides likelihood-free inference. We validate the engine against exact stationary distributions and an independent implementation, show that growth and division alone reproduce published results on cell-size scaling, partitioning noise, lineage-versus-population statistics and cell-cycle-dependent bursting, and demonstrate that a single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces the signatures of drug-tolerant persisters reported in recent experiments and models: dose-dependent decay with unchanged division times, correlated fates of related cells, preserved or collapsed barcode diversity, loss of resistance when memory is disrupted during exposure, an intermediate-dose optimum for drug-induced tolerance, and stable inheritance of a selected phenotype. Used as a ground-truth generator, the framework evaluates memory-gene detection, network inference under the confounds of growth and sequencing, imputation and perturbation-prediction baselines; used as an inference engine, it recovers kinetic and drug parameters from snapshot and lineage data and quantifies the bias of ignoring division. Calibrated to published time-lapse measurements of cisplatin-treated cells, the persister model predicts the fates of cells at a held-out concentration, the biphasic kill curve, the extent of lineage correlations and the dose-invariance of single-cell timing; used as a schedule testbed with a built-in optimiser, it reproduces the outcomes of continuous versus intermittent BRAF/MEK inhibition in the SWOG S1320 trial and of intermittent dosing in xenografts, attributes their difference to the fitness of resistant cells without drug, and evaluates standard against dose-dense temozolomide regimens with pharmacokinetics. Biomodelling.jl 2.0 is open source (MIT) at https://github.com/ayoublasri/Biomodelling.jl.
keywords: [stochastic simulation, gene expression noise, cell division, drug-tolerant persisters, single-cell RNA-seq, lineage tracing, approximate Bayesian computation, Julia]
