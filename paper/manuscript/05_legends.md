# Figure legends

![](fig1_framework.png)

**Figure 1. The Biomodelling.jl 2.0 framework.** (a) Each cell runs the stochastic kinetics of a reaction network (mass-action, Hill or custom propensities, explicit promoter states) at its current volume; volume grows exponentially, genes replicate at a set point of the cycle, and division follows a sizer, adder or timer rule with binomial partitioning of molecules and inheritance of promoter states. (b) Population control (constant size, free growth or logistic), the drug and perturbation layer (dose schedules, state-dependent death, growth inhibition, rate modulation, genetic perturbations) and the lineage table that records every cell's ancestry, fate and state at birth and division. (c) Observation models (scRNA-seq with capture, depth, dropout and batch effects; smFISH; time-lapse reporters) and outputs (CSV, Newick, AnnData). (d) Downstream uses: lineage statistics, fluctuation tests, clonal memory scores, likelihood-free inference and benchmark generation. (e) A complete example in ten lines of code.

![](fig2_engine.png)

**Figure 2. The engine is exact and agrees with an independent implementation.** (a) Stationary distribution of a birth-death process (bars: 50 000 simulated cells; line: Poisson). (b) Telegraph model in an intermediate and a bursty regime against the Beta-Poisson law. (c) Bursty protein production against the negative binomial. (d) Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance to the exact law for every kernel (n = 20 000; dotted line: 99% critical value) with wall-clock time per 10⁴ cells. (e) Telegraph model simulated with Biomodelling.jl and with JumpProcesses.jl on the same model; the two-sample KS statistic is below its 99% critical value. (f) Runtime of population simulations against the number of cells (10 genes) and genes (1 000 cells) for the exact and hybrid kernels.

![](fig3_growth.png)

**Figure 3. Growth, division and partitioning generate memory, size scaling and cell-cycle effects.** (a) A single lineage: volume, mRNA and protein of a slowly switching gene, with the promoter-on state shaded. (b) mRNA count against cell volume for a fast-switching gene without replication (points, line: mean concentration × V) and with gene replication at mid-cycle (binned means). (c) Correlation of protein concentration at division between mothers and daughters, sisters and cousins as a function of the promoter switching rate; dotted: one over the cell-cycle time. (d) Memory timescale in time units against the measured cell-cycle time for two switching rates; dotted lines: 1/(k_on + k_off). (e) Coefficient of variation squared of a stable protein's concentration across the population and along single lineages, for increasing partitioning noise and cell-cycle-time noise. (f) Telegraph parameters fitted to cells before replication (one gene copy), after replication (two copies) and pooled; dotted: per-allele values.

![](fig4_persisters.png)

**Figure 4. A single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces persister biology.** (a) Population size under continuous, pulsed and reduced-dose schedules (shading: drug present). (b) Kill curves at increasing dose. (c) Net population decay rate over the first 30 time units of treatment against dose, with the mean division time of cells born under drug (inset), which is unchanged. (d) Probability that a cell's lineage survives the treatment (at least one descendant alive at the end of the run) given that its sister's or cousin's lineage survived, against the marginal survival probability, for cells born in the 25 time units before the drug; memory gene and fast-switching control, with the fold enrichment above each bar. (e) Effective number of clones (barcodes) before and after drug for pre-existing and drug-induced tolerance (mean ± s.d. of three seeds). (f) Surviving clones after drug without memory disruption, with promoter switching accelerated 20-fold from two cell-cycle times before the drug until the drug arrives, and with the acceleration maintained during exposure (mean ± s.d. of five seeds). (g) Fraction of high-expressing cells before, during and after a drug pulse for slow and fast switching (MGMT-like phenotypic selection). (h) Net population growth rate over a 200-time-unit course as a function of release period (time units without drug between 20-time-unit exposures) and dose, for pre-existing tolerance without and with a fitness cost of the resistant state and for drug-induced tolerance; the box marks the most effective schedule.

![](fig5_benchmarks.png)

**Figure 5. Ground-truth benchmarks.** (a) Clonal variance score against the known memory time of 40 independent genes, from true concentrations and from sequenced counts; dotted: cell-cycle time. (b) Area under the precision-recall curve of correlation (Pearson, Spearman) and GENIE3 network inference on the same 30-gene network observed at fixed volume, in a dividing population (raw counts, concentrations, and concentrations regressed on the cell-cycle covariates copy number and age) and after sequencing (raw and library-size normalised counts); dotted: random ranking. (c) Effect of kNN-smoothing and MAGIC imputation before inference. (d) Coefficient of determination of predicted log₂ fold changes after knocking down the five most connected regulators, for a no-change baseline and for propagation of observational correlations.

![](fig6_inference.png)

**Figure 6. Inference with the forward model.** (a) Posterior distributions of telegraph parameters from ABC-SMC on a snapshot of non-dividing cells, with the truth and the exact maximum-likelihood estimate. (b) The same for a snapshot of a growing and dividing population: the naive telegraph fit is biased while division-aware ABC recovers the truth. (c) Posterior particles for the drug death rate and protection threshold inferred from a kill curve and sister fate concordance. (d) Posterior predictive kill curves simulated at the posterior median against the observed curve.
