# Discussion

Biomodelling.jl 2.0 fills a gap in the single-cell toolkit: a simulator in which expression memory, cell-size effects, cell-cycle dependence and drug-tolerant persistence follow from explicit biophysics instead of being assumptions of a generative model. It is worth separating what is new here from what it confirms. No individual ingredient is new, and the claim is not that any of them is: stochastic kinetics in growing and dividing cells have been simulated before [@bertaux2018; @piho2025abm; @lasri2022], lineage-tracing data have their own simulators [@pan2022tedsim; @jones2020cassiopeia], compartment models of adaptive therapy established that the benefit of treatment holidays depends on a cost of resistance and on turnover [@zhang2017adaptive; @strobl2021], and phenotypic-switching theory established that perturbing the retention of a resistant state can decide a treatment outcome [@gunnarsson2020]. What is new is the combination: to our knowledge no published package couples these mechanisms to joint inference in a single forward model, with a schedule optimiser on top of it. That is a claim about the absence of a tool, which cannot be established exhaustively, and it is made against the packages surveyed in Table 1 and the comparison in Supplementary Note 10; it would be answered by any one package that provides the whole set. Version 1 of this framework already simulated stochastic gene networks in growing and dividing cells and generated synthetic scRNA-seq for imputation and network-inference benchmarking [@lasri2022]; what version 2 adds to it is gene replication and cell-cycle copy number, division by sizer, adder or timer rules rather than a timer alone, inherited promoter states, the lineage table and its statistics, the drug and pharmacokinetic layer with state-dependent killing, and likelihood-free inference. A schedule optimiser built on the same forward model, and the clinical case studies it supports, are reported separately. Relative to the closest external comparator, an agent-based framework that already couples stochastic kinetics to growth, adder division, partitioning and inference from lineage data [@piho2025abm; @piho2024feedback], what is added is the drug and schedule layer, the observation models, gene replication, and the benchmark-generation use. Two consequences follow from the combination rather than from any part of it. It makes a resistance phenotype a heritable expression state with measurable kinetics instead of a compartment with an assumed rate, and it supplies ground truth of a kind observational data cannot: the same simulation gives known memory genes, known networks, known perturbation effects and the true lineage tree, which is what the quantification of how growth and sequencing destroy network inference, and of how far cell-cycle regression recovers it, depends on. It also makes the calibrate-then-predict protocol available for treatment questions, which turns a mechanism into a falsifiable prediction, and makes the failure of that protocol informative in the same currency: the parameters the cisplatin fates do not determine are named quantities that an experiment can go and measure. That difference has practical consequences. As a hypothesis engine for non-genetic drug tolerance, the framework can be used to compare schedules and memory-modulating pretreatments by the long-term fitness they leave in a treated population, with cell-level rules that can be matched to time-lapse and lineage-tracing data. As a ground-truth generator, it produces data with known memory genes, known networks, known perturbation effects and known lineage trees, which is what MemorySeq-style scores, lineage-aware models and virtual-cell benchmarks need in order to be evaluated fairly, and what existing simulators cannot supply. As a likelihood-free inference engine, it estimates mechanistic parameters from data that are now routinely collected, while quantifying the bias of simpler models.

The cisplatin fit sets out a workflow for in silico single-cell pharmacology and, more usefully, shows where it stops. Declare the training and validation observables before fitting, fit by simulated moments or approximate Bayesian computation with common random numbers, validate on conditions and statistics that were not fitted — and then report what the data left open. Here that is a great deal. Six parameters fitted to six fate fractions have no residual degrees of freedom, so the fit cannot be tested for goodness; it does not reach the precision of the measurement; the memory of the resistant state is held near a value taken from lineage correlations in a different cell line, not recovered from the fates; and a hazard gated to the replication window fits the same fractions as well as a heritable expression state does. None of that is a failure of the exercise. It is the exercise. A phenomenological model fitted to the same six numbers would report a fit and stop, because it has no mechanisms to be undetermined between; a forward model that names its mechanisms can say which of them the data reach and which they do not, and can name the experiment that would separate them, which here is a lineage-resolved measurement with the cell-cycle phase recorded alongside the fate.

Several limitations delimit this release. Cells are well-mixed compartments without spatial structure or cell-cell signalling; the pharmacology is a one-compartment model; growth is exponential with phenomenological size control; and the inference layer, although general, relies on user-chosen summary statistics, so posterior calibration should be checked with predictive simulations. The persister case study is deliberately minimal, a single resistance gene with slow switching, and its calibration used fate fractions transcribed from a published study rather than raw tracks. Its death hazard was first written without cell-cycle dependence, which matters for a drug such as cisplatin whose lethality is enhanced during replication. Refitting with a cycle-dependent hazard shows that the two mechanisms are not separable by these data: a model in which killing is confined to the replication window accounts for the fate fractions as well as heritable protective expression does, and the fraction of the hazard that is cycle-independent is not determined by them at all. Cell-cycle position at the moment the drug arrives is therefore a live alternative to inherited protection, and the experiment that distinguishes them is a cycle reporter read at drug addition rather than more fate counts. Cells draw their interdivision times independently, so cell-cycle duration is not inherited, and every fate correlation between related cells in these simulations is carried by the inherited promoter state. That is a real restriction and not only a simplification. Cell-cycle duration is heritable over several generations in mammalian cells, and its inheritance alone produces strong correlations between cousins and second cousins even where mother-daughter correlations are weak [@sandler2015; @kuchen2020; @chakrabarti2018]; combined with a cycle-gated hazard it is a complete competing explanation for the second-cousin fate correlations that the calibration in Fig. 7 reproduces, and the model as built cannot generate it, so the fit has no choice but to load all of that correlation onto expression memory. Chromatin states or cycle timing with their own inheritance rules would let the two be separated, and are the extension this points to. Six parameters fitted to six fractions leave the switching rates weakly determined, so the memory of the resistant state rests on the lineage correlations used as a prior rather than on the fates themselves; those correlations, and the kill-curve plateau the model fails to reproduce, are properties of HCT116 cells, whereas the fate fractions fitted are of U2OS cells, in which only one daughter per division was tracked and the population decay is not biphasic, so the agreement with the correlations is a consistency check across cell lines rather than an independent validation, and the absent plateau may be a difference between the lines as much as a shortcoming of the model; and that memory is carried by one slow promoter, a coarse proxy for chromatin-based inheritance whose single-exponential, symmetric form is a simplification of a process that can be multi-timescale and bistable. A bridge to the Catalyst.jl ecosystem is not part of this release. The benchmark results should be read as demonstrations of what the framework makes possible rather than as verdicts on the methods compared, which were run with default settings on small networks.

Spatial coupling through neighbourhood-dependent rates, cell-cell communication, chromatin states with their own inheritance rules and neural simulation-based inference are natural extensions of the existing forward model; the spatial half of that list is the province of agent-based tumour simulators such as PhysiCell and PhysiBoSS [@ghaffarizadeh2018; @poncedeleon2023], which model tissue mechanics and microenvironment but not stochastic transcription in dividing cells, so the two are complementary rather than competing. Because the framework is deterministic given a seed, fast enough to simulate tens of thousands of cells in minutes, and writes AnnData files, it can be dropped into single-cell analysis pipelines as a source of mechanistically grounded synthetic data, and into virtual-cell training and evaluation as a source of causal, out-of-distribution contexts.
