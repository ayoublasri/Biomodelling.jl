Isogenic cells differ in their transcriptional states, those differences are often inherited across several divisions, and the inherited state biases what a cell does under stress. Cancer cells that survive targeted or cytotoxic therapy frequently do so without a resistance mutation: they occupy a rare, reversible, drug-tolerant expression state that pre-exists the treatment or is induced by it [@sharma2010; @shaffer2017; @hata2016; @oren2021; @franca2024]. Lineage-resolved experiments now measure this memory directly. The fates of sisters and cousins are correlated, clonal barcode diversity is preserved or collapsed depending on whether tolerance pre-exists or is induced, and the timescale of expression fluctuations relative to the cell cycle decides which genes carry memory at all [@iyer2025; @lin2025memory; @ghosh2025powerseek; @harmange2023]. In parallel, mechanistic analyses of single-cell RNA-sequencing data have shown that transcriptional kinetics can be inferred correctly only when gene copy number, cell age and partitioning at division are modelled explicitly [@sukys2025; @zhang2025; @okochi2026scdivide], and evaluations of "virtual cell" models for perturbation prediction keep finding that deep-learning approaches fail to beat simple baselines, largely for want of mechanism and causal structure [@ahlmanneltze2025; @vollenweider2026; @dibaeinia2026context; @bunne2024].

These developments create a demand that existing simulators do not meet. The simulators used to benchmark single-cell methods divide according to whether they supply a regulatory network as ground truth, and neither kind models cell growth, volume-dependent transcription, gene replication or the partitioning of molecules at division. Splatter and scDesign3 draw counts from statistical models fitted to reference data, and SymSim draws them from a two-state promoter whose kinetic parameters are products of cell-specific and gene-specific factors; none of the three supplies a network [@zappia2017; @song2024scdesign3; @zhang2019symsim]. SERGIO, dyngen and scMultiSim simulate the kinetics of a user-specified network [@dibaeinia2020; @cannoodt2021; @li2025scmultisim], and GRouNdGAN imposes one on an adversarially trained generator, so that each gene's counts follow from those of its transcription factors [@zinati2024groundgan]. Most of these simulators place cells on a differentiation trajectory, and lineage simulators such as TedSim and Cassiopeia [@pan2022tedsim; @jones2020cassiopeia] generate division trees, but the expression attached to those trees is phenomenological and largely Markovian. Expression memory, cell-size scaling, cell-cycle-dependent bursting and persister dynamics therefore have to be imposed on them rather than emerging, and none provides the ground truth that the methods now being developed for exactly those phenomena require [@pratapa2020]. Stochastic simulators of growing and dividing cells do exist, and the most complete of them couples reaction kinetics to growth, division and inference from lineage data [@bertaux2018; @piho2025abm; @piho2024feedback; @thomas2021]; what none of them adds is a drug and schedule layer, observation models for the measurements these questions are asked of, and benchmark generation. Agent-based tumour simulators supply the drug response and the spatial structure but not stochastic transcription in dividing cells [@ghaffarizadeh2018; @poncedeleon2023].

Here we present Biomodelling.jl 2.0, a rewrite of a Julia framework whose distinguishing feature, the coupling of stochastic reaction kinetics to exponential growth, volume-scaled transcription and binomial partitioning at division [@lasri2022], is precisely the mechanism behind heritable expression states. Version 2.0 turns that feature into a complete framework for the mechanistic study of drug tolerance and for single-cell benchmarking. We validate the engine against exact stationary laws and an independent implementation, show that growth, division and partitioning alone reproduce published results on cell-size scaling, partitioning noise, lineage-versus-population statistics and cell-cycle-dependent bursting, and demonstrate that a single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces the quantitative lineage and memory signatures of heritable drug tolerance. We then use the framework as a ground-truth generator for memory-gene detection, network inference, imputation and perturbation prediction; as an inference engine that recovers kinetic parameters from snapshot and lineage data, quantifies the bias of ignoring division, and maps the ridge to which drug parameters are determined by population data; and as a model fitted to published time-lapse measurements of cisplatin-treated cells, where the exercise is less a validation than a map of what those measurements determine: the fit is not within measurement error, the memory of the resistant state rests on a prior rather than on the data, and a cycle-gated hazard is an equally good explanation. Reporting that is the point. A forward model whose mechanisms are named can be shown not to be identified by the data it fits, and the schedule optimiser the framework also provides is only worth using on parameters that experiments have pinned down.
