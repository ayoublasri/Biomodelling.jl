Isogenic cells differ in their transcriptional states, those differences are often inherited across several divisions, and the inherited state decides what a cell does under stress. Cancer cells that survive targeted or cytotoxic therapy frequently do so without a resistance mutation: they occupy a rare, reversible, drug-tolerant expression state that pre-exists the treatment or is induced by it [@sharma2010; @shaffer2017; @hata2016; @oren2021; @franca2024]. Lineage-resolved experiments now measure this memory directly. The fates of sisters and cousins are correlated, clonal barcode diversity is preserved or collapsed depending on whether tolerance pre-exists or is induced, and the timescale of expression fluctuations relative to the cell cycle decides which genes carry memory at all [@iyer2025; @lin2025memory; @ghosh2025powerseek; @harmange2023]. In parallel, mechanistic analyses of single-cell RNA-sequencing data have shown that transcriptional kinetics can be inferred correctly only when gene copy number, cell age and partitioning at division are modelled explicitly [@sukys2025; @zhang2025; @okochi2026scdivide], and evaluations of "virtual cell" models for perturbation prediction keep finding that deep-learning approaches fail to beat simple baselines, largely for want of mechanism and causal structure [@ahlmanneltze2025; @vollenweider2026; @dibaeinia2026context; @bunne2024].

These developments create a demand that existing simulators do not meet. The simulators used to benchmark single-cell methods, among them SERGIO, dyngen, scMultiSim and GRouNdGAN [@dibaeinia2020; @cannoodt2021; @li2025scmultisim; @zinati2024groundgan], model regulation and technical noise but not cell growth, volume-dependent transcription, gene replication or the partitioning of molecules at division; lineage simulators such as TedSim [@pan2022tedsim] generate division trees but attach phenomenological, largely Markovian expression to them. None of them can therefore produce expression memory, cell-size scaling, cell-cycle-dependent bursting or persister dynamics from first principles, and none provides the ground truth that the methods now being developed for exactly those phenomena require [@pratapa2020]. Stochastic simulators of growing and dividing cells exist [@bertaux2018; @piho2025abm] but stop at the single-cell state: they offer no drug response, no lineage statistics, no observation models, no inference and no benchmark generation.

Here we present Biomodelling.jl 2.0, a rewrite of a Julia framework whose distinguishing feature, the coupling of stochastic reaction kinetics to exponential growth, volume-scaled transcription and binomial partitioning at division [@lasri2022], is precisely the mechanism behind heritable expression states. Version 2.0 turns that feature into a complete framework for the mechanistic study of drug tolerance and for single-cell benchmarking. We validate the engine against exact stationary laws and an independent implementation, show that growth, division and partitioning alone reproduce published results on cell-size scaling, partitioning noise, lineage-versus-population statistics and cell-cycle-dependent bursting, and demonstrate that a single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces the quantitative signatures of drug-tolerant persisters. We then use the framework as a ground-truth generator for memory-gene detection, network inference, imputation and perturbation prediction; as an inference engine that recovers kinetic and drug parameters from snapshot and lineage data and quantifies the bias of ignoring division; as a model calibrated to published time-lapse measurements and validated on conditions it was not fitted to; and, with a built-in schedule optimiser, as a testbed in which clinical dosing regimens are compared and better ones are sought.
