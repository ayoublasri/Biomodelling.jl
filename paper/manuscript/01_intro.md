# Introduction

Single-cell technologies have made non-genetic heterogeneity a central object of study in cell biology and drug discovery. Isogenic cells differ in their transcriptional states, these states are often inherited across several divisions, and the inherited states decide cell fate under stress: drug-tolerant persister cells that survive targeted or cytotoxic therapy arise from pre-existing or drug-induced expression states rather than from mutations [@shaffer2017; @shaffer2020; @harmange2023; @iyer2025; @lin2025memory]. Lineage-resolved single-cell experiments now quantify this memory directly, showing that the fates of sisters and cousins are correlated, that clonal barcode diversity is preserved or collapsed depending on whether tolerance pre-exists or is induced, and that the timescale of expression fluctuations relative to the cell cycle determines which genes carry memory [@iyer2025; @lin2025memory; @ghosh2025powerseek]. In parallel, mechanistic analyses of single-cell RNA-sequencing data have established that transcriptional kinetics can only be inferred correctly when gene copy number, cell age and partitioning at division are modelled explicitly [@sukys2025; @zhang2025], and evaluations of "virtual cell" models for perturbation prediction have found that deep-learning approaches still fail to beat simple baselines, largely because they lack mechanism and causal structure [@ahlmanneltze2025; @vollenweider2026; @dibaeinia2026context].

These developments create a demand that existing simulators do not meet. Simulators used to benchmark single-cell methods, such as SERGIO, dyngen, scMultiSim and GRouNdGAN [@dibaeinia2020; @cannoodt2021; @li2025scmultisim; @zinati2024groundgan], model regulation and technical noise but not cell growth, volume-dependent transcription, gene replication or the partitioning of molecules at division; lineage simulators such as TedSim [@pan2022tedsim] generate division trees but attach phenomenological, largely Markovian expression to them. As a consequence none of them can produce expression memory, cell-size scaling, cell-cycle-dependent bursting or persister dynamics from first principles, and none provides the ground truth needed to test the methods that are now being developed for exactly those phenomena. Stochastic simulators of growing and dividing cells exist [@bertaux2018; @piho2025abm] but stop at the single-cell state: they do not provide drug response, lineage statistics, observation models, inference or benchmark generation.

Biomodelling.jl was introduced as a generator of synthetic scRNA-seq data from stochastic gene regulatory networks in growing and dividing cells, and was used to benchmark imputation methods for network inference [@lasri2022]. Its distinguishing feature, the coupling of stochastic reaction kinetics to exponential growth, volume-scaled transcription and binomial partitioning at division, is precisely the mechanism behind the heritable states discussed above. Here we present version 2.0, a rewrite that turns this feature into a complete framework for the mechanistic study of heritable expression states, drug tolerance and single-cell benchmarking. The package adds (i) a typed model layer with mass-action, Hill and custom kinetics and explicit promoter states; (ii) exact, hybrid and adaptive stochastic kernels validated against analytic laws and an independent implementation; (iii) a population layer with sizer, adder and timer division control, binomial or over-dispersed partitioning, promoter inheritance, gene replication and constant-size, free-growth or logistic population control; (iv) a drug and perturbation layer with dose schedules, pharmacokinetics, state-dependent death, growth inhibition, drug-induced rate changes and genetic perturbations; (v) complete lineage records with heritability, autocorrelation, fluctuation-test and clonal-memory statistics; (vi) observation models for scRNA-seq, smFISH and time-lapse reporters with AnnData output; and (vii) likelihood-free inference and the exact telegraph likelihood.

We validate the engine against exact stationary distributions and against JumpProcesses.jl, show that growth, division and partitioning alone reproduce published results on cell-size scaling, partitioning noise, lineage-versus-population statistics and cell-cycle-dependent bursting, and demonstrate that a single resistance gene with slow promoter switching reproduces the quantitative signatures of drug-tolerant persisters reported in recent experimental and theoretical work: dose-dependent population decay with unchanged single-cell division times, correlated fates of related cells, preserved or collapsed barcode diversity, loss of resistance when memory is disrupted during exposure, an intermediate-dose optimum for drug-induced tolerance, and stable inheritance of a selected phenotype. We then use the framework as a ground-truth generator to evaluate memory-gene detection, network inference under the confounds of growth and sequencing, imputation, and perturbation-prediction baselines, show that the same forward model supports parameter inference from snapshot and lineage data and quantifies the bias incurred by ignoring division, calibrate the persister model to published time-lapse data and validate it on held-out conditions, and finally use it, with a built-in schedule optimiser, to evaluate clinical dosing regimens and to search for better ones.
