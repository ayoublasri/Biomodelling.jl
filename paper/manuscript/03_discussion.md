# Discussion

Biomodelling.jl 2.0 fills a gap in the single-cell toolkit: a simulator in which expression memory, cell-size effects, cell-cycle dependence and drug-tolerant persistence follow from explicit biophysics instead of being assumptions of a generative model. It is worth separating what is new here from what it confirms. The individual ingredients are not new: stochastic kinetics in growing and dividing cells have been simulated before [@bertaux2018; @piho2025abm; @lasri2022], lineage-tracing data have their own simulators [@pan2022tedsim; @jones2020cassiopeia], compartment models of adaptive therapy established that the benefit of treatment holidays depends on a cost of resistance and on turnover [@zhang2017adaptive; @strobl2021], and phenotypic-switching theory established that perturbing the retention of a resistant state can decide a treatment outcome [@gunnarsson2020]. Three things are new. First, the combination itself: molecular kinetics, volume-scaled transcription, gene replication, partitioning and promoter inheritance, drug schedules with state-dependent killing, a complete lineage record, observation models and inference in one forward model, which is what allows a resistance phenotype to be a heritable expression state with measurable kinetics rather than a compartment with an assumed rate. Second, the ground truth this makes available: the same simulation supplies known memory genes, known networks, known perturbation effects and the true lineage tree, which is what the quantification of how growth and sequencing destroy network inference, and of how far cell-cycle regression recovers it, depends on. Third, the calibrate-then-predict protocol applied to schedule questions, which turns a mechanism into a falsifiable prediction: here it identifies the growth of resistant cells without drug and their residual growth under drug as the two properties that decide whether holidays help, and points to the rate at which temozolomide consumes MGMT as the measurement that would separate the two glioblastoma regimens. That difference has practical consequences. As a hypothesis engine for non-genetic drug tolerance, the framework answers which schedule, which combination or which memory-modulating pretreatment minimises the long-term fitness of a treated population, with cell-level rules that can be matched to time-lapse and lineage-tracing data. As a ground-truth generator, it produces data with known memory genes, known networks, known perturbation effects and known lineage trees, which is what MemorySeq-style scores, lineage-aware models and virtual-cell benchmarks need in order to be evaluated fairly, and what existing simulators cannot supply. As a likelihood-free inference engine, it estimates mechanistic parameters from data that are now routinely collected, while quantifying the bias of simpler models.

The calibration and schedule studies set out a workflow for in silico single-cell pharmacology: declare the training and validation observables before fitting, fit by simulated moments or approximate Bayesian computation with common random numbers, validate on conditions and statistics that were not fitted, and only then compare regimens, reporting outcomes that map onto clinical end points. The case studies show why mechanism matters. The same question, continuous or intermittent dosing, has opposite answers depending on two properties of the resistant cells, their growth under drug and their fitness without it, and a trial result and a xenograft result that appeared to contradict each other are both reproduced once those properties are set — set in each case rather than inferred from the outcomes, so the case studies show that the ranking follows from cell properties, not that any particular tumour has them. Because the framework simulates the lineage-resolved data from which such properties are measured, it can also say which experiment would settle the question before a trial is designed.

Several limitations delimit this release. Cells are well-mixed compartments without spatial structure or cell-cell signalling; the pharmacology is a one-compartment model; growth is exponential with phenomenological size control; and the inference layer, although general, relies on user-chosen summary statistics, so posterior calibration should be checked with predictive simulations. The persister case study is deliberately minimal, a single resistance gene with slow switching, and its calibration used fate fractions transcribed from a published study rather than raw tracks. Its death hazard was first written without cell-cycle dependence, which matters for a drug such as cisplatin whose lethality is enhanced during replication. Refitting with a cycle-dependent hazard shows that the two mechanisms are not separable by these data: a model in which killing is confined to the replication window accounts for the fate fractions as well as heritable protective expression does, and the fraction of the hazard that is cycle-independent is not determined by them at all. Cell-cycle position at the moment the drug arrives is therefore a live alternative to inherited protection, and the experiment that distinguishes them is a cycle reporter read at drug addition rather than more fate counts. Six parameters fitted to six fractions leave the switching rates weakly determined, so the memory of the resistant state rests on the lineage correlations used as a prior rather than on the fates themselves; and that memory is carried by one slow promoter, a coarse proxy for chromatin-based inheritance whose single-exponential, symmetric form is a simplification of a process that can be multi-timescale and bistable. The clinical case studies use literature values for cell-cycle times, resistant fractions and pharmacokinetics rather than patient-specific measurements, so their conclusions concern mechanisms and rankings, not absolute survival times. In the glioblastoma study MGMT is consumed stoichiometrically, one molecule per lesion repaired, so its depletion follows the cumulative exposure as a suicide enzyme's should; what remains unmeasured is the rate constant of that consumption, which is why we name it as the quantity that separates the regimens. MGMT is in any case only one determinant of a temozolomide response that is also mismatch-repair-dependent, and the methylated and unmethylated MGMT fractions are illustrative values that bracket low and high expression rather than measured quantities. The memory of the resistant state is expressed in these tumour-scale studies as a number of net population doublings, not of cell divisions, and so is not the division-counted memory of memory-sequencing experiments. A bridge to the Catalyst.jl ecosystem is not part of this release. The benchmark results should be read as demonstrations of what the framework makes possible rather than as verdicts on the methods compared, which were run with default settings on small networks.

Spatial coupling through neighbourhood-dependent rates, cell-cell communication, chromatin states with their own inheritance rules and neural simulation-based inference are natural extensions of the existing forward model. Because the framework is deterministic given a seed, fast enough to simulate tens of thousands of cells in minutes, and writes AnnData files, it can be dropped into single-cell analysis pipelines as a source of mechanistically grounded synthetic data, and into virtual-cell training and evaluation as a source of causal, out-of-distribution contexts.
