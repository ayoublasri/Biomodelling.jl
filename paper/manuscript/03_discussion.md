# Discussion

Biomodelling.jl 2.0 occupies a niche that the single-cell field currently lacks: a mechanistic simulator in which expression memory, cell-size effects, cell-cycle dependence and drug-tolerant persistence are consequences of explicit biophysics rather than assumptions of a generative model. Three uses follow. First, as a hypothesis engine for non-genetic drug tolerance, it lets one ask which schedule, which combination or which memory-modulating pretreatment minimises the long-term fitness of a treated population, with cell-level rules that can be matched to time-lapse and lineage-tracing data. Second, as a ground-truth generator, it produces data with known memory genes, known networks, known perturbation effects and known lineage trees, which is what methods such as MemorySeq-style scores, Power-Seek, lineage-aware models and virtual-cell benchmarks require to be evaluated fairly, and which existing simulators cannot provide. Third, as a likelihood-free inference engine, it allows mechanistic parameters to be estimated from the kinds of data that are now routinely collected, while quantifying the bias of simpler models.

The calibration and schedule studies illustrate the workflow we propose for in silico single-cell pharmacology: declare the training and validation observables before fitting, fit by simulated moments or ABC with common random numbers, validate on conditions and statistics that were not fitted, and only then compare regimens, reporting outcomes that map onto clinical end points (time to progression from the nadir, depth of response, cumulative dose). The two case studies show why mechanism matters: the same schedule question, continuous or intermittent dosing, has opposite answers depending on whether resistant cells pay a fitness cost without drug, and the trial and xenograft outcomes that seemed to contradict each other are both reproduced once that property is set. Because the framework simulates the lineage-resolved, single-cell data from which such properties are measured, it can also say which experiment would resolve the question before a trial is designed.

Several limitations delimit the current release. Cells are well-mixed compartments without spatial structure or cell-cell signalling; the pharmacology is a one-compartment model; growth is exponential with phenomenological size control; and the inference layer, although general, relies on user-chosen summary statistics, so posterior calibration should be checked with predictive simulations as in Figure 6. The persister case study is deliberately minimal, a single resistance gene with slow switching; its calibration used fate fractions transcribed from a published study rather than raw tracks, and the clinical case studies use literature values for cell-cycle times, resistant fractions and pharmacokinetics rather than patient-specific measurements, so their conclusions are about mechanisms and rankings, not about absolute survival times. The Catalyst.jl bridge planned for interoperability with the SciML ecosystem is not part of this release. Finally, the benchmark results should be read as demonstrations of what the framework makes possible rather than as verdicts on the methods compared, which were run with default settings on small networks.

Future work will add spatial coupling through neighbourhood-dependent rates, cell-cell communication, chromatin states with their own inheritance rules, and neural simulation-based inference on top of the existing forward model. Because the framework is deterministic given a seed, fast enough to simulate tens of thousands of cells in minutes, and outputs AnnData files, it can be dropped into existing single-cell analysis pipelines as a source of mechanistically grounded synthetic data, and into virtual-cell training and evaluation as a source of causal, out-of-distribution contexts.
