# Discussion

Biomodelling.jl 2.0 fills a gap in the single-cell toolkit: a simulator in which expression memory, cell-size effects, cell-cycle dependence and drug-tolerant persistence follow from explicit biophysics instead of being assumptions of a generative model. That difference has practical consequences. As a hypothesis engine for non-genetic drug tolerance, the framework answers which schedule, which combination or which memory-modulating pretreatment minimises the long-term fitness of a treated population, with cell-level rules that can be matched to time-lapse and lineage-tracing data. As a ground-truth generator, it produces data with known memory genes, known networks, known perturbation effects and known lineage trees, which is what MemorySeq-style scores, lineage-aware models and virtual-cell benchmarks need in order to be evaluated fairly, and what existing simulators cannot supply. As a likelihood-free inference engine, it estimates mechanistic parameters from data that are now routinely collected, while quantifying the bias of simpler models.

The calibration and schedule studies set out a workflow for in silico single-cell pharmacology: declare the training and validation observables before fitting, fit by simulated moments or approximate Bayesian computation with common random numbers, validate on conditions and statistics that were not fitted, and only then compare regimens, reporting outcomes that map onto clinical end points. The case studies show why mechanism matters. The same question, continuous or intermittent dosing, has opposite answers depending on whether resistant cells pay a fitness cost without drug, and a trial result and a xenograft result that appeared to contradict each other are both reproduced once that property is set. Because the framework simulates the lineage-resolved data from which such properties are measured, it can also say which experiment would settle the question before a trial is designed.

Several limitations delimit this release. Cells are well-mixed compartments without spatial structure or cell-cell signalling; the pharmacology is a one-compartment model; growth is exponential with phenomenological size control; and the inference layer, although general, relies on user-chosen summary statistics, so posterior calibration should be checked with predictive simulations. The persister case study is deliberately minimal, a single resistance gene with slow switching, and its calibration used fate fractions transcribed from a published study rather than raw tracks. The clinical case studies use literature values for cell-cycle times, resistant fractions and pharmacokinetics rather than patient-specific measurements, so their conclusions concern mechanisms and rankings, not absolute survival times. A bridge to the Catalyst.jl ecosystem is not part of this release. The benchmark results should be read as demonstrations of what the framework makes possible rather than as verdicts on the methods compared, which were run with default settings on small networks.

Spatial coupling through neighbourhood-dependent rates, cell-cell communication, chromatin states with their own inheritance rules and neural simulation-based inference are natural extensions of the existing forward model. Because the framework is deterministic given a seed, fast enough to simulate tens of thousands of cells in minutes, and writes AnnData files, it can be dropped into single-cell analysis pipelines as a source of mechanistically grounded synthetic data, and into virtual-cell training and evaluation as a source of causal, out-of-distribution contexts.
